Beyond Japan: The Global Rise of Anime

Growth of Anime Industry Overseas

Overseas Profit Surpassing the Domestic Anime Industry

The anime industry is made up of revenue generated by anime production companies through production costs, licensing rights, and other anime-related businesses. It also includes revenue from anime-related products and services, such as merchandise, streaming, movies, music, and live events. As shown by the orange portion of the stacked bar chart, the overseas anime market has experienced significant growth over the years, eventually surpassing the domestic Japanese market in revenue. This highlights how anime has expanded beyond Japan and become a major global entertainment industry.

Overseas vs Domestic Market

The slope chart compares the growth of Japan's domestic and overseas anime markets between 2015 and 2024. During this period, the overseas market experienced significant growth, increasing from ¥282.3 billion to ¥2,170.2 billion, representing an average annual growth rate of approximately 25.4%. In comparison, the domestic market grew at a much slower rate of 3.8% per year. The much steeper upward trajectory of the overseas market compared to the domestic market further highlights the growing significance of international audiences to the anime industry. With overseas revenue surpassing domestic revenue by 2024, this trend demonstrates how anime has evolved from a primarily Japanese entertainment industry into a major global market.

Which countries purchase anime licensing?

What this map shows

As anime continues to grow in popularity worldwide, more countries are purchasing licensing rights to distribute Japanese animation. The world map shows the number of anime licensing contracts across different countries, with darker colours representing a higher number of licensed works. As shown in the map, countries such as China, the United States, and several Southeast Asian countries have a strong demand for Japanese anime. Asia accounts for the largest share of overseas licensing contracts, followed by Europe and North America. This highlights how anime has expanded beyond Japan and become an important part of entertainment and popular culture around the world.

Anime impact on Pop-Culture

Anime has generated significant economic value while also shaping popular culture and bringing people together through conventions, screenings and fan communities.

Who goes to Anime Conventions?

The waffle chart shows the age distribution of attendees at SMASH! Chibi 2025, highlighting how anime is particularly popular among teenagers and young adults. While younger generations make up the majority of attendees, approximately 25% are in their early 30s, with 3.9% aged 40 and above. This suggests that anime appeals to a wide range of age groups, including long-time fans who grew up watching anime as it gained popularity internationally from the 1970s onwards. Overall, this highlights anime's lasting cultural influence and its ability to bring together fans from different generations through conventions and social events.

Most Enjoyed Series?

The bump chart shows how the top 10 highest-rated anime on MyAnimeList (MAL) have changed between 2020 and 2026. Fantasy and adventure anime appear to be particularly popular, with titles such as *Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood*, *Hunter x Hunter*, and *Sousou no Frieren* consistently ranking highly. Notably, *Sousou no Frieren* overtook *Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood* in 2024 and maintained its #1 position through 2026. This highlights the strong appeal of fantasy and adventure genres among anime fans, while also showing how long-standing favourites continue to remain highly rated despite the release of newer series.
